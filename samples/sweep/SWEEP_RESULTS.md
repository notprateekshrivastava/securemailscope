## What this adds, and what it does not

It adds **coverage**: every protocol against every port family against every TLS profile,
including the `static-rsa` profile which no hand-written lab capture used. It does not add
new *kinds* of weakness - the profiles are the same ones the lab set uses.

It also adds **sessions to train on**: the 21 captures on TShark-recognised ports (465, 993,
995) each yield one mail session. The 21 on high ports (9465, 9993, 9995) exercise the
opposite path: implicit TLS with no protocol vocabulary to read, so the protocol inside
cannot be proven and the stream is reported as UNCLASSIFIED rather than guessed from a port.

## Reading the UNCLASSIFIED rows

They are not failures of the sweep - the captures are valid and the conversations took
place. They demonstrate the documented limit: when a client's first byte is a TLS
ClientHello and the port is not one TShark recognises, a passive capture contains no
evidence of which protocol is inside. Guessing from the port number would be inventing
evidence, so the analyzer reports what it can prove and stops there.
